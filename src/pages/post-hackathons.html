How to Win Your First Hackathon: A Playbook

By NextGR Editorial · 7 min read ·

Hackathons reward teams that ship a clear, working demo of a focused idea — not the most complex code. Here's the playbook.

1. Build a balanced team of 3–4

You want a builder (back end / AI), a front-end or design person, and a storyteller who owns the pitch and research. Solo is possible; four is the sweet spot for most events.

2. Read the judging criteria first

Most events score on some mix of impact, technical difficulty, creativity, completeness and presentation. Also check sponsor prizes — they are often less crowded than the grand prize.

3. Choose a tiny, painful problem

“AI for education” is too broad. “A tool that turns a teacher's lecture recording into a 5-question quiz in 30 seconds” is winnable in 48 hours.

4. Scope ruthlessly

  • Hours 0–4: problem, user, one core flow, sketch.
  • Hours 4–30: build only the happy path. Fake what doesn't matter.
  • Final 6 hours: freeze features, polish UI, record a backup demo video, write the README.

5. Win the demo

Use a 3-minute structure: the problem as a real person's story (30s), live demo of the core flow (90s), how it works (30s), what's next and the ask (30s). Always have the recorded demo ready in case Wi-Fi fails.

Judges remember one sentence. Decide what yours is before you start building.

6. After the event

Publish the project, write a short post about what you built and connect with judges and sponsors. Hackathon projects are some of the strongest portfolio pieces for internships.
